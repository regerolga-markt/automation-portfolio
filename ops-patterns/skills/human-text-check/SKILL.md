# Human text check

Applies to every text the user will publish or send under their own name: LinkedIn posts,
emails, messages, cover letters, CV bullets, GitHub READMEs, bios, comments. Works for
English, Russian and German text.

Also applies when the user pastes their own text and asks "does this sound like AI?" or
"make it sound less like AI".

The goal is text that reads like a specific practitioner wrote it quickly and knew what they
meant. Not text that was polished until it had no fingerprints.

## Process

1. Draft the text as usual (with whatever writing skill fits the task).
2. Run the checklist below on the draft. Every hit gets rewritten, not just softened.
3. Re-read the result once more as a sceptical reader (for example a recruiter who has seen
   500 ChatGPT posts this month). If a sentence could appear in any of them, rewrite or cut it.
4. Deliver the final text. Under it, one short line on what was removed (e.g. "removed:
   question hook, triplets, closing moral"). No long report unless asked.

When checking the user's own text: list only the concrete hits with a proposed replacement for
each. Do not rewrite parts that are fine. Deliberate style choices are not errors.

## Checklist: structure

- **Rhetorical question as the opener** ("How do you...?", "Sound familiar?"). Replace with a statement of fact.
- **Setup → reveal with a colon or "The result?"** ("Here's the thing:", "The answer? Simple.", "The outcome?").
- **"Not X, but Y" / "It's not about X, it's about Y" / "This isn't just X, it's Y".** Say Y directly.
- **Triplets everywhere.** Three adjectives, three parallel clauses, three short sentences in a row. One triplet per text maximum, only when there really are three things.
- **Perfectly parallel bullets**, each starting with a bold label and a colon, each the same length. Vary them or turn them into prose.
- **Moral at the end** ("That turned out to be the most useful part", "And that's the real lesson", "The takeaway is simple"). End on the last concrete point instead.
- **Symmetry**: every paragraph the same length, every section with the same shape.
- **Over-signposting**: "Let's dive in", "Here's what I learned", "Let's break it down", "Let's take it step by step".
- **Summary of what was just said** ("In short", "To sum up", "Thus").
- **Generic closing question** ("What do you think?", "How do you handle this?" when nobody would answer).

## Checklist: words and punctuation

- **English tells:** delve, tapestry, landscape, navigate (figuratively), robust, seamless, crucial, pivotal, foster, elevate, unlock, harness, game-changer, in today's fast-paced world, it's worth noting, at the end of the day, a testament to, journey, leverage, empower, transform.
- **Russian tells:** the local equivalents of "it is important to note", "it is worth emphasizing", "in the modern world", "key", "unique", "comprehensive approach", "it's no secret that", "plays an important role", the formal copula "is/represents" where a plain "is" works, and bureaucratic officialese in general.
- **German tells:** the local equivalents of "in today's fast-paced world", "it is important to emphasize", "exciting journey", "holistic", "creating added value".
- **Em dashes.** Max one per text. Replace the rest with a period, comma or parentheses.
- **Emoji arrows and pointers** (👇 🚀 ✅). Remove unless the user uses them themselves.
- **Hedging stacks** ("might potentially help", "may to some extent").
- **Intensifiers without content** ("incredibly", "truly", "really", "genuinely").

## Checklist: content

- **Abstract claims without a detail.** Every claim needs a tool, a number, a timeframe or a mechanism. "Improved reporting" → "the watchdog alerts if Monday's data load is stale".
- **Invented feelings and insights.** Do not add "I was surprised", "it was harder than I expected", "the most useful part" unless the user said it. Fabricated emotion to sound human is worse than no emotion. If a line like that is needed, ask.
- **Invented numbers or results.** Never. Use the mechanism instead.
- **Everything explained.** Real practitioners skip obvious context. Cut sentences a peer would find patronising.

## What human text usually has

- Uneven rhythm: a long sentence, then a short one. Not a pattern.
- Plain verbs: built, rebuilt, broke, moved, logged, cut.
- Specific nouns from the actual work: Make scenario, Windsor API, search terms report, GTM container.
- A slightly abrupt ending.
- Occasional first-person reasoning ("I rebuilt it because the operations bill kept growing").

## Don't

- Don't swap one set of AI phrases for another set of "human-sounding" clichés.
- Don't add typos, slang or fake casualness. Match the user's register (for professional writing, that is usually business, not colloquial).
- Don't pad the text with a long explanation of the check.
